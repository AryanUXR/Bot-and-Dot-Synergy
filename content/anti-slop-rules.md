# Anti-Slop Writing Rules

Apply to all prototype copy before it goes into any Lovable prompt.
These rules apply equally to persona traits, about paragraphs, dot descriptions, hero headings, taglines, and CTA text.

## The six standing rules

1. **Say the specific thing.** Replace the category with the instance. Not "financial support" — "Mudra Kishore loan up to ₹5 lakh". Not "faces challenges" — "her R400 in savings is not enough to cover the food trader permit fee".

2. **Cut every adjective that doesn't add information.** "Leading", "trusted", "premier", "dedicated", "passionate", "committed to" — delete all of them. If the adjective could be removed without changing the meaning, it was not doing work.

3. **One idea per sentence.** When a sentence has two ideas joined by "and", split it. "She has the skill and she needs clients" → "She has the skill. She has no clients yet."

4. **Avoid passive voice.** "Support is provided by" → "They provide". "Applications are accepted from" → "Apply if". Rewrite every passive.

5. **No imperative titles or slogans.** Button text and headings do not give orders. Not "Discover Your Future" — "Find providers near you". Not "Unlock Opportunity" — anything except that.

6. **No em dashes.** Never. Rewrite the sentence instead. "She sells out every morning — three shops want her supply" → "She sells out every morning. Three shops want regular supply but she cannot commit yet."

## Five recurring failures to catch

### 1. Flowery language
BAD: "Kgomotso's entrepreneurial spirit shines through her daily hustle"
GOOD: "Kgomotso sells out of fat cakes every morning at Seshego taxi rank"

### 2. Vague language
BAD: "She needs support to grow her business"
GOOD: "She needs a food trader permit and a shared kitchen to take on three spaza shop orders"

### 3. Unnecessary words
BAD: "In order to be able to access the services she needs"
GOOD: "To access the services she needs"

BAD: "Due to the fact that"
GOOD: "Because"

### 4. Illogical constructions
BAD: "Her journey of empowerment begins with a single call"
GOOD: Delete. Never write this sentence.

### 5. Awkward phrasing
BAD: "She is someone who is in the process of trying to figure out"
GOOD: "She is figuring out"

## Absolute banned list

Never use any of the following in prototype copy:

```
em dash (—)         → rewrite the sentence
semicolons          → split into two sentences
"it's not X, it's Y" constructions
"passionate about"
"dedicated to"
"committed to"
"empowerment" / "empower"
"journey" (as a metaphor)
"unlock" (as a metaphor)
"discover" (as a CTA)
"impact"
"leverage" (as a verb)
"ecosystem"
"holistic"
"solution" (generic)
"seamlessly"
"robust"
```

## Dots-specific rules

### For persona traits (bullet strings)
- Each trait = one concrete fact. Age. Location. What they have. What they don't have. What they need.
- Never: "Passionate about building her future in [sector]"
- Always: "Has Electrical Engineering N6, no tools, no clients yet"

### For persona `about` paragraphs
- Paragraph 1: Who they are. Where. What they've done. One specific achievement or situation.
- Paragraph 2: What blocks them. The gap between where they are and where they need to go. Make it concrete.
- Paragraph 3: What this call is for. What they need Raya/Siza/Saarthi to help them with. One clear ask.

### For dot descriptions (provider/organisation descriptions on the map)
- Maximum 2 sentences.
- Sentence 1: What the organisation is and what it does. Just facts.
- Sentence 2: The specific value for this seeker type. A programme name, a loan limit, a language spoken, a distance advantage.
- No superlatives. No mission statements.

### For hero headings
- Name the personas. Don't describe them generically.
- BAD: "Explore voices from across the community"
- GOOD: "Two seekers. One mentor. Three journeys."

### For taglines
- Geography + what the bot does. Under 8 words.
- BAD: "Empowering livelihoods across India"
- GOOD: "Your livelihoods guide, Limpopo" or "MSME support, Leh district"
- Say what the bot does (helps you find), never what it provides. BAD: "Work, a room and child care near you" (sounds like Sakhi hands them out). GOOD: "Helps you find hostels, child care and jobs in Tiruppur".
