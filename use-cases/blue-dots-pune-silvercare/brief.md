# Blue Dots Pune · Silver economy (elder care): use case brief

**Source:** Aryan, 16 Nov 2026.
- **The problem:** Pune has home care agencies, day-care centres for elders, home nurses, physiotherapists and trained caregivers. Families still find them through word of mouth, usually in a crisis, and often from another city. Trained caregivers find work the same way.
- **Before:** "My mother lives alone in Pune and I'm in Bengaluru. When she needed help, I didn't know whom to trust or where to start."
- **After:** "One call, and I knew two checked attendant services a few minutes from her home."

**Fit check (`counsel/SKILL.md`): fits.**
- **Discovery, not supply:** the services already exist.
- **Two sides with intent:** elders and families seek care; agencies, centres, nurses and caregivers provide it, and caregivers also look for work.
- **Strongly local:** "nearby" is measured from the elder's home.
- **Short to say:** a need fits a one-minute call.

Health and care is an area for exploration, with no evidence yet from a live implementation (KD p.20). The closest proof is Purple Dots (KD p.8–9).
